The business problem
02

The director's questions

“You are the analyst for an animal shelter. The director wants to know how animals come in, what happens to them, and where to act.”

How do animals come in?

Volume, intake type and seasonality — what demand the shelter faces and how it has changed.

What happens to them?

Adoption, return to owner, transfer and other outcomes — measured per visit, not per record.

Where should we act?

Patterns the shelter can influence, backed by evidence strong enough to defend.

Source data

  • Intakes: 173,812 records · 01-10-2013 to 04-05-2025
  • Outcomes: 173,775 records · 01-10-2013 to 05-05-2025
  • Public City of Austin open-data extract; 2013 and 2025 are partial years

Assignment scope

  • A 2–3 page Power BI report using both datasets
  • A model linking intakes to outcomes, a date table and length of stay per visit
  • Trend with year-over-year comparison; slicers for animal type, intake type and date
  • Three findings the director should act on
Frame the brief: three questions, two datasets, partial first and last years. The mandatory report elements are covered in the Power BI demo; today's focus is the third question — where to act.

Why the analytical tables differ from the source. I audited and cleaned the originals, removed exact duplicates, standardised dates and types, and rebuilt individual visits. The Power BI model is built on those visit and event tables.

Approach
05

Analytical approach

Each question is answered at the right level of detail, with definitions fixed before looking at results.

Two levels of analysis

Events and visits

Events answer volume questions — how many animals came in or left. Visits answer journey questions — how long, and how it ended. Event shares are never treated as conversion rates.

Length of stay

Intake to final recorded outcome

Measured on matched visits only. Medians are used because stays are highly skewed: median 6.00 days, 90th percentile 55.56 days. It is not confirmed kennel time — foster periods are not recorded.

Time comparisons

Like-for-like periods

The extract ends 05-05-2025, so 2013 and 2025 are partial. Year-over-year uses complete years only; findings compare a 2014–2019 baseline with recent years.

Share of those dog visits lasting more than 30 days
Why it matters

Intake events fell 40% (19,726 in 2019 → 11,815 in 2024), yet the estimated average number of animals between intake and outcome fell only 18% (1,120 → 916). Longer stays are absorbing the capacity freed by lower intake.

Recommendation — a long-stay pathway for dogs.
Review every dog at day 14 and day 30 with a named placement plan (adoption promotion, foster, partner transfer). Track the share of dogs over 30 days monthly as an operating KPI.
Interpretation. This is time from intake to the final recorded outcome. Foster placement dates are not recorded, so shelter-only and foster time cannot be separated. The trend holds when foster-subtype outcomes are excluded.
What it does not show. The data identifies the change, not its cause. The next step is to break long stays down by intake type, age and outcome category.
Lead with 5.12 → 12.08 (pooled median — say the statistic). Robustness: non-foster 4.95 → 8.94, >30 days 8.67% → 22.58%. Capacity argument. Recommendation, then caveat.

6,668 · 4,058 · 2,441 visits. Of the returns, 60.86% happened within 30 days of adoption.

Definition

An owner-surrender visit whose previous matched visit for the same animal at this shelter ended in adoption. Time is measured from that adoption to the surrender intake.

Recommendation — support adopters in the first 30 days.
Structured check-ins around day 3 and day 14, a behaviour-support line, and a return-prevention conversation before a surrender is accepted. Measure the 30-day return share each month.
Why it matters. Most returns happen early, when support is most likely to help — and every avoided return is a kennel that stays free.
Interpretation. A prior adoption does not explain why an animal was surrendered — surrender reasons are not recorded. Adoptions before October 2013 are invisible, so 2016 onward is used.
24.46% any prior adoption; 14.89% within 30 days; 8.96% within 7 days — all shares of owner surrenders, 2016–2024. Early concentration = window for support. Caveat: reason unknown.

Recommendation — reunify at the door.
Scan every stray dog for a microchip at intake, post found dogs the same day, check owner-contact data quality, and promote the City's free microchip and ID-tag service. Track return-to-owner share alongside field returns.
Why it matters. Every reunification ends a stay within days, frees capacity quickly and is the best outcome for the owner.
Interpretation. Dogs returned to owners in the field, without a shelter intake, are not in this dataset. A changed mix of dogs reaching the shelter could also lower the share. Verify against operational records before treating it as performance.
29.46% → 12.51%; absolute 2,036 → 386. RTO is the fastest exit. Recommendation: reunify at the door. Field-return blind spot — be explicit.
Context
09

Welfare, policy and environmental context

External context frames the findings. It is kept separate from what the data shows.

State law

Texas Health & Safety Code ch. 823 — shelters must meet housing and sanitation standards, separate animals by species, sex and size, and keep healthy animals apart from sick ones; an annual veterinary inspection is required.1

Safe Outdoor Dogs Act (SB 5, 2021) — restrained dogs need adequate shelter, shade and drinkable water; chains are prohibited.2

City ordinance & policy

Stray hold — about three days (72 hours) before a stray can be adopted or transferred; owner surrenders transfer to the City immediately.3

95% live-outcome commitment (2019); a 2024 code change excludes wildlife such as coyotes, raccoons and bats from that reporting.4

Official findings & guidance

City Auditor, Sept 2023 — the shelter's welfare priorities conflict, and it frequently limited intake because of space.5

Public health — bats and skunks are typically the main rabies sources in Texas6; CDC advises shade, fresh water and never leaving pets in cars in extreme heat.7

What our data shows

Longer dog stays (Finding 1) bear directly on space — the pressure the audit describes.

87% of wildlife-intake visits end in euthanasia, mostly recorded as rabies risk — consistent with the public-health context, and the reason wildlife is reported separately.

Hypothesis only

Heat and weather may affect welfare before intake; the dataset records no diagnoses or exposures, so this is not a finding.

Action plan
10

From records to action

Priority 1 · capacity

Dog long-stay pathway

Day-14 and day-30 case reviews with a placement plan.

Monitor: share of dog visits over 30 days (no foster subtype) — baseline 22.58% for 2023–2024.

Priority 2 · prevention

First-30-days adopter support

Early check-ins, behaviour support and return-prevention conversations.

Monitor: share of owner surrenders adopted within the previous 30 days — baseline 14.89% for 2016–2024.

Priority 3 · reunification

Reunify at the door

Microchip scans at intake, same-day posting, owner-contact data quality.

Monitor: return-to-owner share of stray-dog visits — baseline 12.51% in 2024 — alongside field returns.

Limits to keep in view. Length of stay is time to the final recorded outcome, not kennel time. Surrender reasons and field returns are not recorded. 2013 and 2025 are partial years. The outcome grouping in the report is provisional, not the City's live-release rate. These are descriptive patterns, not causes.
Data that would sharpen the picture. Foster start and end dates, recorded surrender reasons, and field reunifications — each would turn a well-supported pattern into a measurable operating metric.

Three findings, three levers: get long-stay dogs moving, support adopters early, and get lost dogs home faster.
